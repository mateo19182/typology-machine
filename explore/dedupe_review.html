Review the saved Coruña sign groups. Judge physical identity: two identical signs at different positions are different objects. A changed sign across years is a case to flag in the notes. These crops may include objects incorrectly detected as signs.

How the current deduplication works, and proposed improvements

The code uses DINOv2 crop embeddings. Its current defaults consider crops from different panoramas when their camera locations are within 30 m and cosine similarity is at least 0.80. Accepted pairs are joined transitively. A matches B and B matches C can merge all three without A matching C. Saved group files do not record run parameters, so these defaults are not verified provenance for every group in this snapshot.

Groups trigger a spatial split when their camera-coordinate range on either axis exceeds 50 m. Splitting uses complete-linkage distances. The axis check can miss a diagonal span above 50 m. The representative maximizes detector score × shorter bounding-box side. Apple and Google are grouped separately. Nothing is deleted.

The new multicategory detector only marks overlapping same-category boxes in the same view as duplicates at IoU ≥ 0.8. It retains every proposal. That is separate from the older cross-panorama grouping reviewed here; the new categories do not yet have cross-panorama groups.

  1. Measure errors before tuning thresholds. Use these decisions to distinguish incorrect merges from missed duplicates. This sample deliberately includes suspicious cases and cannot estimate city-wide accuracy.
  2. Handle overlapping views of one panorama. Match projected boxes or viewing rays. The same-panorama exclusion misses direct matches, while transitive links can still put same-panorama crops together. Those may be legitimate overlapping views or different objects. Test an explicit cannot-merge constraint for clearly separate, non-overlapping objects in the same source view, accounting for fragmented detections.
  3. Constrain group joins. Test a minimum similarity to the representative or a group-consistency rule to prevent long visual chains. Keep uncertain matches separate.
  4. Add geometry and verified category. Camera proximity is only candidate selection. Use compatible viewing direction and object position where reliable; account for GPS error. Repeated bins or identical traffic signs especially need geometry. Avoid a hard gate on unverified detector categories.
  5. Preserve dates and object history. Separate repeated views of an unchanged object from a replacement at the same location. Do not merge a business's different sign faces just because their design matches.
  6. Make each decision inspectable. Save match edges, scores, rejection reasons, model revision and grouping parameters. Use stable object IDs; current sequential group IDs can change on rerun. Keep source-specific observations when later matching Apple to Google.

One inspected example is Apple group s18500: 250 crops, including a roofline business sign and several distinct red storefront text panels. The weakest pair has cosine similarity 0.142 despite belonging to one group. This is a concrete case to review when deciding whether our unit should be a sign panel or an entire storefront; the current stated goal is individual physical signs.

Suggested first change after review: fix overlapping-view duplicates and chain merges, then test geometry. Do not globally lower the similarity threshold without checking the false merges it creates.

Snapshot and sample coverage

This snapshot covers the existing grouped crops, not every newly collected detection. The running multicategory job is not part of these saved cross-panorama groups.

Includes largest groups, weak matches from a random subset, random multi-view groups, and strong nearby pairs from a bounded search. Groups larger than 36 crops show a subset including the representative and weakest pair. Judge the shown crops; a “same” answer does not certify unshown members. “Weakest” is an all-pairs comparison inside a group, not necessarily an edge the algorithm used to merge it.
